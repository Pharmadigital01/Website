Drug-Food Interactions (Visual Clinical Guide)

Key diet restrictions and biochemical mechanisms affecting bioavailability & toxicity.

HIGH RISK

Atorvastatin / Simvastatin + Grapefruit

Mechanism: Furanocoumarins in grapefruit juice inhibit intestinal CYP3A4 enzyme.

Clinical Effect: Increases statin plasma levels by up to 300%, escalating rhabdomyolysis and severe muscle damage risk.

💡 Counseling: Avoid grapefruit completely or consult pharmacist for non-CYP3A4 statin options (e.g. Pravastatin).
MODERATE RISK

Warfarin + Green Leafy Veggies (Spinach/Kale)

Mechanism: High Vitamin K content directly antagonizes Warfarin's inhibition of Vit K epoxide reductase.

Clinical Effect: Drastically lowers INR value, predisposing patient to arterial thrombosis or stroke.

💡 Counseling: Maintain a consistent daily Vitamin K intake; avoid sudden spikes in leafy greens consumption.
MODERATE RISK

Lisinopril (ACEi) + Bananas / Potassium Supplements

Mechanism: ACE Inhibitors decrease aldosterone secretion, impairing renal potassium excretion.

Clinical Effect: Excess potassium buildup leads to severe hyperkalemia and potential fatal cardiac arrhythmias.

💡 Counseling: Limit high-potassium diet overload; monitor serum K+ levels regularly.
HIGH RISK

Doxycycline / Tetracyclines + Milk & Dairy

Mechanism: Divalent Calcium ions (Ca2+) form insoluble chelate complexes with antibiotic molecules.

Clinical Effect: Decreases GI drug absorption by up to 60%, causing clinical treatment failure.

💡 Counseling: Take antibiotic 2 hours before or 4 hours after consuming dairy or calcium antacids.
SEVERE / CRITICAL

MAO Inhibitors + Aged Cheese (Tyramine)

Mechanism: MAO-A inhibition blocks breakdown of dietary Tyramine in the gastrointestinal tract.

Clinical Effect: Triggers massive norepinephrine release causing acute hypertensive crisis.

💡 Counseling: Strictly avoid fermented cheeses, cured meats, and draft beer while on MAOIs.

Pharmacovigilance & Naranjo Causality Scale

Standardized clinical score calculation for Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) causality assessment.

Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction Probability Scale

1. Are there previous conclusive reports on this reaction?

2. Did the adverse event appear after the suspected drug was administered?

3. Did the adverse reaction improve when the drug was discontinued?

4. Did the adverse reaction reappear when the drug was re-administered?

5. Are there alternative causes that could have caused the reaction?

6. Did the reaction reappear when a placebo was given?

7. Was the drug detected in blood/fluids in toxic concentrations?

8. Was the reaction more severe when dose was increased?

9. Did the patient have a similar reaction to the same drug previously?

10. Was the adverse event confirmed by objective evidence?
